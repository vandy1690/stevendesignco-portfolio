## Why the link is the headline, not the card

The obvious build is to wrap the whole card in an anchor. It was built that way
first, and it was wrong.

A link's name comes from its content. Wrapping everything means the link
announces the headline, the paragraph, both meta labels and their values, and
the call to action, as one long string. Replacing that with a written label is
worse: the name then no longer matches the words on screen, which breaks voice
control and was flagged by an audit.

The stretched link solves both. The headline is the link, so its name is exactly
the visible headline. An invisible layer extends its click area over the card, so
nothing changes for a mouse. Hover and the focus ring move to the card through
`:has()`, so the card still responds as one object.

## Anatomy

**Art** sits at the top, 16 by 9, full bleed, cropped to fill. A logo lockup is
the exception: it fits inside the box instead of cropping, because cropping a row
of marks cuts marks off.

**Headline** is display type and carries the link. It is the argument, not a
label. "Nobody asked for this one." not "Merchant Flow Builder".

**Text** is one or two sentences of support.

**Meta** is two pairs, Product and Objective. Not Client and Service: the work is
described by what it was and what it was for, not by who paid.

**Call to action** reads "Read case study" and is plain text, not a second link.
Two links to the same place in one card is a duplicate for anyone navigating by
links.

## Content limits that are real

Card height is capped so the deck can pin a card to the viewport. Copy that runs
long does not overflow; it trips a fit check and drops the whole deck into the
plain list fallback. The Plate card is 18 pixels over on a 600px-tall phone
today, which is why that size gets the list.

Keep the text to two sentences. Keep the objective under about six words.

## Accessibility

The card's accessible name is the headline alone. The arrow in the call to
action is `aria-hidden`, because "arrow" in a name is noise.

Art is decorative: `alt=""` always. The headline already says what the card is
about, so describing the picture repeats it.

## Checks before you ship

1. A screen reader announces the card as its headline and nothing else.
2. Clicking the picture opens the case study.
3. Tab to it: the ring is around the card, not just the headline text.
4. At 402 by 660 the deck still scrolls rather than falling back to a list.
