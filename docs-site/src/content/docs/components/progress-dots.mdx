## How position is decided

The dots read the same scroll value the deck already computes, so there is no
second scroll listener.

For the fan of recommendations, the active dot is the card nearest the centre.
For the flip deck of case studies, the dot moves once the incoming card has
passed edge-on, not when the scroll begins. That means the dot always matches
the card you can actually read, rather than running ahead of the animation.

## Why they are hidden from screen readers

The cards are already a list in the accessibility tree, and the page offers a
keyboard route to every case study. A position indicator on top of that is
noise, not information. Position by shape and tone is a convenience for people
watching the animation.

## Where they disappear

The row is removed, not just faded, in four situations, all of which replace the
deck with a plain list:

- The visitor asked for reduced motion.
- The viewport is under 600px tall, which is also what 400 percent zoom looks like.
- A runtime check found a card does not fit, so the deck degraded.
- JavaScript did not run.

## A trap worth knowing

The dot row costs vertical space inside a stage that is already tight on phones.
The first attempt took that space from the card height cap, which pushed the two
longest cards past their limit and tripped the fit check, dropping common phone
sizes into the list fallback. The stage already had the room. If you change the
dot row's height or spacing, re-run the phone height checks in
[Degradation](/docs/practices/degradation/) before shipping.

			<li>Make them clickable.</li>

			<li>Use them on a section that shows all its items at once.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>

## Checks before you ship

1. Scroll the deck. The filled dot matches the card you can read, not the one arriving.
2. Turn on reduced motion. The row is gone and every card is visible.
3. At 402 by 660, the deck still scrolls and the dots still show.
