## Why one treatment for both

How I work lists tools. Recognition lists awards. They are different content
with the same shape: a name you might scan for, and a line explaining it to
someone who would not recognise the name.

Using one treatment for both is a deliberate signal that they are the same kind
of thing. Giving awards a fancier style would be the site congratulating itself.

## Content

The key is a proper noun or a short phrase. The description is one sentence, no
full stop needed, explaining the thing to someone outside the field.

For awards, explain what the award is rather than which piece won it. "The Neals
are the top honour in American business media" tells a reader from another
industry what they are looking at.

On phones the two columns collapse to one, key above description.

<div class="sdc-dd">
	<div class="sdc-dd--do">
		<h4>Do</h4>
		<ul>
			<li>Keep descriptions to one sentence.</li>
			<li>Explain a name a stranger would not know.</li>
			<li>Keep the list to six items or fewer.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
	<div class="sdc-dd--dont">
		<h4>Don't</h4>
		<ul>
			<li>Style recognition differently from tools.</li>
			<li>Put a paragraph in the description column.</li>
			<li>Use it for two items; use prose instead.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
</div>

## Checks before you ship

1. Every row has both a key and a description.
2. At 390px wide the description sits under its key, not beside it.
3. No description runs past two lines on desktop.
