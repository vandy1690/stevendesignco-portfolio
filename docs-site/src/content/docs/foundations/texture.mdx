## What it is

A one pixel dot on a twenty pixel grid, drawn as a single background image on
the body. Not an overlay element, not an image file, no extra request. Turn it
up or down with one token.

It comes from an open-source component that was a black dot grid at 35 percent
opacity. At that strength on a warm ground it read as polka dots and fought the
cards, so the geometry was kept and the colour moved onto the site's own ink at
10 percent.

## Where it appears and does not

The page ground has it. Cards do not: a card is a surface sitting on the page,
and the flat fill is what makes it read as lifted.

The case study dialog does not have it either, which is one of the three reasons
the same case study looks slightly different opened in place versus visited
directly.

The generated card artwork carries its own paper texture at a slightly different
off-white. That is deliberate, not a mismatch: the images are opaque panels, not
a continuation of the page.

## Contrast implications

Text sitting over the grid is technically sitting over two colours. The dot is
about 4.4 to 1 against the old hard-coded body grey, which is one of the reasons
that grey was replaced with the muted token.

If you darken the dot, re-check body copy contrast against the dot, not just
against the ground.

## Checks before you ship

1. Cards still read as lifted, with no grid inside them.
2. Body copy over the grid still clears 4.5 to 1 against the dot.
