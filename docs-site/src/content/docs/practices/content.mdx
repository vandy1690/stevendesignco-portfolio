## Voice

Plain language over jargon. Short sentences. One idea each.

**No em dashes or en dashes.** Anywhere, including inside quotes.

**No contractions in professional writing.** The site relaxes this where a
sentence would otherwise read stiffly; application materials do not.

**Sentence case** for headings.

## The accuracy rule

**Never publish an unconfirmed claim. Ask instead.**

Every number on this site traces to something checkable. Where a figure is
remembered but not verified, it stays off until a source confirms it.

This has teeth. A statistic about mobile traffic was left off a case study
because the only record of it was a recollection. It is still off.

## No fabricated artifacts

No AI-generated image that imitates a real artifact, ever. Generated art may be
decorative and abstract. It may not pretend to be a screen, a document or a
piece of work that existed.

A generated image once carried invented magazine cover lines that read as real
typography. Reading them closely showed words that were not words. It was not
published.

## Proprietary work

PayPal work is shown generically. No flows reproduced, no plugin interface, no
internal names, no board images. Only merchant names on the cleared list may
appear.

## Barred phrases

A specific list lives in the repo's content guide. Each entry is there because
it was once wrong, unprovable, or overstated. Some examples: a payments volume
acronym, claims that a tool was adopted by a whole team, per-flow timing
numbers, and a claim about picking up a framework quickly.

Check the list before writing new copy. It is short, and it is absolute.

## Length limits are functional

Several components clip or change layout when copy runs long. The case study
card is the strict one: two sentences. Past that, the deck may drop to its
fallback list on smaller screens.

## Checks before you ship

1. No em dash or en dash.
2. Every number traces to a source you can name.
3. No barred phrase.
4. Card copy is two sentences or fewer.
